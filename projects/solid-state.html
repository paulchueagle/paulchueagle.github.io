Research Summary

This research modeled the electronic structure and local magnetic moment distribution in dimerized polymer chains using a modified Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model. The SSH model is a tight-binding framework that describes one-dimensional conducting polymers (like polyacetylene) by accounting for the coupling between electron hops and lattice vibrations (phonons).

Our simulation examined a 50-site polymer chain under the influence of electron-phonon coupling (α), lattice displacement (un), on-site electron-electron interactions (U), and nearest-neighbor interactions (V). By self-consistently solving the Hamiltonian matrix, we modeled the local magnetic moment m(i) at each atom site i. We discovered that for a weak electron-phonon coupling (α = 0.25), the magnetic moment undergoes spatial damping, dropping from its maximum at the boundaries down to a stable, flat value of 0.008 in the center of the polymer, creating a symmetrical butterfly-like spatial profile.

Simulation Result (m)
Damping is highly symmetric, converging towards the core value of 0.008
  • Damping Effect: For V = 0, the magnetic moment is maximum at the chain boundaries (m ≈ 0.016) due to boundary state pinning, and decays rapidly to m = 0.008 at the center.
  • Neighbor Interactions: Increasing the neighbor potential V suppresses the boundary enhancement. At V = 1.0, the profile is almost completely flat, signifying a uniform charge/spin distribution.
  • Oscillatory Sawtooth Behavior: In addition to the envelope damping, the moment exhibits slight sawtooth alterations between odd and even lattice sites, reflective of the dimerized lattice structure.
